# HealthGraph — Campaign Architecture

Owner: STRATEGY agent. Source of truth: `campaign/00-brief.md` (master brief). Product truth: `references/screen-inventory.md`.
This document binds hooks, CTAs, thumbnails, localization, and distribution for all 20 reels (EN + DE) and the discovery film.

## 1. Creative rationale (one line)

The whole campaign is one argument made twenty times: **scattered health information becomes connected context — and the graph, built from real screens, is the proof.**

Every reel is a different door into that same argument: a pain door (fragmentation), a curiosity door (ask your records), a craft door (documents → timeline), a beauty door (3D graph). No reel promises virality; each reel is engineered for one primary viewer outcome (see §6) and a distinct CTA (see §4).

## 2. Clusters and entry-point logic

| Cluster | Reels | Door (organic discovery entry point) |
|---|---|---|
| A — Fragmentation | 01, 03, 10, 17 | Pain: "my data is everywhere." Universally shared experience; the fastest in for cold audiences. |
| B — AI / records | 02, 06, 11, 18 | Curiosity: "you can ASK your records?" Demonstrates the AI chat on verified screens; answers the "is this just another chatbot?" objection with evidence. |
| C — Health Diary | 05, 08, 14 | Emotion: your days as paper reports; the physical archive is the warmest, most rewatchable content. |
| D — Documents / structure | 04, 16 | Credibility: the technical spine (doc → extraction → structured event → timeline). Earns trust of the detail-oriented viewer. |
| E — Measurements | 12, 13 | Utility: one reading means little; over time it means something. PPG pulse screen as proof, explicitly without diagnosis. |
| F — Medication | 07 | Specificity: one concrete, high-frequency pain (medication as history, not reminders). |
| G — Voice | 15 | Novelty: the red voice FAB, a journal that answers back; strongest "follow for more" energy. |
| H — Vision / brand | 09, 19, 20 | Awe: the category statement, the cinematic 3D, and the full overview; these carry brand + site intent. |

Entry-point logic: a stranger who sees ONE reel learns (a) the pain, (b) the category ("the health graph app"), (c) one proof, (d) the trust line ("Kostenlos · DSGVO-konform · Ohne Werbung" in DE). The 20 reels are permutation of those four beats; clusters decide which door the stranger walks through first. Discovery into the profile should never require two reels to make sense: each reel names the product and ends with one CTA.

## 5. German localization strategy

**Tone.** Precise, trustworthy, modern, calm, privacy/data-conscious. The German viewer is the person who reads the Datenschutzerklärung. Narration should speak like a good pharmacy clerk: short sentences, no shouting, no exclamation marks, numbers as proof, the graph shown instead of described. Trust framing where the repo strategy supports it: DSGVO-first posture, "Kostenlos · DSGVO-konform · Ohne Werbung" as standing end-card line, "Deine Daten" phrasing (your data, your device, your graph).

**Sentence-length pacing.** German compounds are long; DE narration must be SHORTER per sentence than EN, not the same words joined. Rules:
- One idea per sentence; max ~14 words per spoken sentence.
- Narration drives scene timing (per brief) — cut beats to sentence, not to word.
- DE reels run ~5–10% slower on the same structure; retime scenes to DE narration, never stretch EN footage timing.
- Subtitles: max 2 lines, max ~18 characters per line on mobile; DE lines break at the compound boundary with a hyphen when they wrap (e.g. "Gesundheits-/geschichte"), never split mid-morpheme without a hyphen.

**Cultural examples (use instead of EN equivalents).**
- EN "inbox" / "files" → DE "Ordner" / "Befundbrief im Briefumschlag" (paper lab letter by post is the shared German experience).
- EN "doctor's office" → DE "Termin beim Hausarzt" (the 12-minute appointment is the cultural frame; never claim medical facts about it).
- EN "journal" → DE "Tagebuch" / "Gesundheits-Tagebuch".
- Paper documents → DE "Akt", "Schublade", "Briefumschlag" — the physical archive is where German health history actually lives.
- "Health Diary" and "HealthGraph" stay as product names in DE copy (proper nouns, not anglicisms). "3D" and "BPM" stay as internationally understood units.

**Forbidden patterns (hard).**
- No anglicisms in narration or subtitles: no "downloaden", "check-in", "logging", "streaming", "feature", "updaten" (use: herunterladen → avoid entirely in favor of neutral phrasing; Eintrag; aktualisieren; Funktion).
- No American hype: no "revolutionär", "Gamechanger", "breakthrough", "the only way", "world's first", exclamation-mark marketing.
- No medical claims: no "diagnostiziert", "Ärztin-Ersatz", "klinisch bewiesen", "Heilung"; wellness posture only; where AI appears, the on-screen "Wellness info only. Not medical advice." line stays visible.
- No fake urgency, no fake social proof (never invent user counts, "X users agree", or review quotes).
- No unverified statistics: no "8 von 10 Apps", no invented percentages. Only real screen values (from the screen inventory) or generic category descriptions.

**Compound-word typography rules.**
- Allow hyphenation across subtitle line breaks only at morpheme boundaries: "Gesundheits- / geschichte", "Daten- / schutzerklärung" → prefer line break after the compound's first part: "Deine / Gesundheitsgeschichte".
- Overlay/thumbnail text: prefer short compounds over stacked compounds ("Gesundheits-App" OK, "Gesundheits-App-Frage" never).
- Numbers: keep the on-screen values exactly as in the screenshots (59 BPM, 67 BPM, 420 min, 977 kcal, 12 nodes · 22 connections); DE number style elsewhere ("3,5" not "3.5"), but never restyle values that appear on real screens.
- "DSGVO" always with capital S, never "DSGVO-konform!" with exclamation.

## 7. Experiment matrix (2nd-hook A/B tests)

Test protocol: run hook A for one full posting cycle; replace only the 0–2s hook (same footage, same subs layout, same CTA); re-post as a fresh reel 5+ days later; compare 2-second hold + completion + saves at comparable reach. One test at a time per cluster. No paid amplification; no identical-hour posting advantage.
